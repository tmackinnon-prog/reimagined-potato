Introduction to Wikipedia

What it is, how it works, and how to use it effectively as a starting point for research.

📖 What Is Wikipedia?

Wikipedia is a free, multilingual online encyclopedia that anyone can edit. Launched in January 2001 by Jimmy Wales and Larry Sanger, it has grown into one of the largest reference works ever created, with more than 60 million articles across hundreds of languages.

The English edition alone contains over 6.7 million articles. It is operated by the non-profit Wikimedia Foundation and is supported entirely by donations and volunteer contributors.

Wikipedia's core philosophy is that knowledge should be free, neutral, verifiable, and collaboratively maintained. Every claim should ideally be supported by a cited, reliable source.

🗺️ Anatomy of a Wikipedia Article

Understanding the layout of a Wikipedia article helps you navigate it efficiently.

Page Section → What It Contains
Lead / Intro
A concise summary of the most important facts. Great for a quick overview.
Table of Contents
Clickable links to each section. Use it to jump to the part you need.
Body Sections
In-depth information organized into headed sections and subsections.
Inline Citations [1]
Superscript numbers linking to a source. These are the sources worth following.
References / Notes
Full bibliographic details for every inline citation. Your research starting point.
Further Reading
Suggested books and articles for deeper study.
External Links
Official websites, databases, or related resources outside Wikipedia.
Talk Page
Editor discussions about the article's accuracy and neutrality.

✅ Strengths

  • Excellent starting point for background knowledge
  • Inline citations link to primary and secondary sources
  • Covers niche topics not found in traditional encyclopedias
  • Articles are regularly updated as events unfold
  • Completely free — no paywall or account required
  • Available in 300+ languages

⚠️ Limitations

  • Anyone can edit — errors can appear before they are corrected
  • Coverage can be uneven (popular topics are more accurate)
  • Bias can exist in contested or political topics
  • Not usually accepted as a citable source in academic work
  • Rapidly changing topics may have outdated information
  • Some articles lack sufficient citations

🔍 How to Use Wikipedia for Research

Wikipedia is best used as a launchpad, not a final destination.

  1. Get the big picture. Read the lead paragraph and skim section headers to build a mental map of the topic.
  2. Identify key terms. Note vocabulary, names, and concepts you can use as search terms in library databases.
  3. Follow the citations. Scroll to the References section and look for books, journal articles, or reputable news sources — those are what you cite.
  4. Check the Talk page. If an article is marked as disputed or biased, the Talk page will explain why and help you evaluate reliability.
  5. Compare across languages. For international topics, the same article in another language can offer a different perspective and additional sources.
Instructor note: Many courses prohibit citing Wikipedia directly in papers. Always check your assignment guidelines — but using Wikipedia to find citable sources is almost always fine.

🏅 Evaluating Article Quality

Wikipedia grades its own articles. Look for the quality label in the top-right corner or on the Talk page:

  • Featured Article (FA) ★ — Wikipedia's highest standard; thoroughly sourced and reviewed by the community.
  • Good Article (GA) — Well-written and reliably sourced, but not yet featured.
  • Start / Stub — Incomplete; treat with extra caution and always verify claims.

You can also check when the article was last edited (bottom of the page) and review its edit history to see how stable or contested it is.

📝 Citing Wikipedia (When Permitted)

If your instructor allows citing Wikipedia, use the "Cite this page" tool in the left sidebar of any article to generate a correctly formatted citation. A typical MLA 9 citation looks like:

"Photosynthesis." Wikipedia, The Free Encyclopedia, Wikimedia Foundation, 12 Apr. 2025, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosynthesis. Accessed 14 Apr. 2026.

Because Wikipedia content can change, always record the access date and, if available, the permanent link (found under "Cite this page") for the exact version you read.

💡 Quick Tips & Keyboard Shortcuts

  • Press Alt + ← / → in your browser to move through article history.
  • Add /simple before .wikipedia.org to access Simple English Wikipedia — great for complex topics.
  • Use the Search bar at the top and check "Did you mean…" suggestions to find the canonical article.
  • Hover over a blue link to get a preview without leaving the page.
  • Wikipedia's sister projects (Wikidata, Wikibooks, Wikisource) are linked at the bottom and can be useful for raw data or primary texts.
